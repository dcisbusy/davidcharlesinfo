Practice: Autumn Gathering

This nature connection practice is incredibly simple and, I find, playful, calming — and occasionally profound.

  1. Set a timer for five minutes (or not) and sit down somewhere in nature. It could be grass, woodland, grassy woodland, beach, riverside, desert, savannah, glacier, the moon, etc.

  2. Gather things within reach. You can choose whether you gather:

    1. Alike things, e.g.: similar fallen leaves, pebbles, twigs.

    2. Unalike things, e.g.: different grasses, leaves, shells, pebbles, twigs.

    3. (You will notice that alike things are also unalike, doesn’t matter — just decide your own definitions.)

  3. When the timer goes off (or when you finally get bored), take stock. Things have been gathered. You have chosen to bring these things together and now here we all are. Count them up. Do a roll call. Marvel at the gathering of things.

If you want to continue this practice, rather than gathering yet more things, I’d suggest you spend time with each individual in your gathering.

Examine them as closely as you can. See how different and how similar they are.

Wonder how they got here. Wonder how you crossed paths in this way. Wonder what brought you to gather this special thing and not some other special thing. Is the thing extra special because you gathered it?

In life, we gather things constantly, both consciously and unconsciously.

Sometimes we forget the richness around us, the diversity, the commonality. Sometimes we forget to simply reach out. Sometimes we forget to gather.

Sometimes we notice we’ve been holding onto something for so long that we’ve forgotten that we ever picked it up. We think that it’s always been there. But it hasn’t.

Autumn is a time for gathering and for taking stock.

Now: scatter all the things you gathered back into the landscape where you found them.

And ask yourself this: what — or who — could you reach out for?
