What are Positive Constraints?

Positive Constraints are things we deliberately choose NOT to do, reaping the benefits of a life without. For example: I haven't taken an aeroplane for five years now. I obviously haven't travelled any less - I've travelled much more, in fact. But I've travelled by hitch-hiking, cycling or on foot - as well as by train, ferry and bus.

Another example: Since Wednesday, I've had no mobile phone. It's only been a couple of days, but already I'm feeling calmer. There is evidence that owning a mobile phone puts us into a state of "continuous partial attention", unable to switch off mentally, which makes us increasingly anxious.

Now you know what they are, I bet you can think of a few Positive Constraints that you already follow. Perhaps you became a vegetarian or don't eat sugar on Mondays; maybe you never work on the Sabbath or took part in Movember and didn't shave for a month. Great! - you know the weird and wonderful things that can happen when you stop doing something that you once thought was crucial to your existence.
